Supporting Your Clients Through a Problem You Didn't Create

As a managing agent, cladding and external wall fire safety wasn't the job you signed up to do, but it's often the job that lands on your desk — a lender querying an EWS1, a freeholder client asking what the exposure is, leaseholders wanting answers you don't have. You're managing the relationship and the day-to-day running of the building; you're not necessarily resourced, or expected, to run a full technical remediation project in-house as well.

That's the gap Wrightwood fills: a specialist partner you bring in for the technical and project-management side of a specific building, while you keep the client relationship and the wider management instruction.

What an Agent Can — and Can't — Take On

It's worth being precise about this, because getting it wrong matters. A managing agent can play an active, valuable role in coordinating a building safety or remediation process — commissioning surveys, liaising with contractors, communicating with leaseholders. What a managing agent generally cannot do is hold the statutory duties that the Building Safety Act and related legislation place on specific legal roles, such as the Accountable Person or Principal Accountable Person for a higher-risk building, or the Responsible Entity for Cladding Safety Scheme purposes. Those responsibilities sit with the building's freeholder, RTM company or resident management company, even where the agent is doing most of the practical work on their behalf.

Not legal advice

This is a general, practical summary, not legal advice. The precise allocation of statutory duties depends on your specific client structure and the building in question — your own or your client's legal advisers are best placed to confirm who holds what responsibility for a particular building.

In practice, this means Wrightwood works alongside the agent and their client, not in place of either — we help make sure the practical coordination work (which agents are well placed to support) and the legal/statutory responsibility (which sits with the Responsible Entity) stay properly aligned, rather than getting blurred.

Why Agents Choose a Specialist Partner

Bringing in a dedicated coordinator for a cladding or remediation project means you're not trying to build a one-off, in-house technical capability for a single building, or stretching your existing team across a type of project most managing agent businesses don't handle often. It also means your client gets a more confident, better-managed process — which reflects well on the agent who brought the right help in, rather than one who tried to handle it alone and lost momentum.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from managing agents.

01

Can a managing agent appoint a consultant to manage cladding remediation?

Yes, a managing agent can commission and coordinate a consultant like Wrightwood to manage the technical process on behalf of their client. What the agent generally cannot do is hold the underlying statutory duties (such as Accountable Person or Responsible Entity status) — those remain with the freeholder, RTM company or resident management company. See our note on what an agent can and can't take on.

02

Who pays for Wrightwood's services — the agent or the client?

This is a commercial matter between you and your client, and we're happy to work however you structure it — typically the cost is agreed with and borne by the Responsible Entity client, often through the service charge, with the agent coordinating the instruction.

03

Can you support multiple buildings across our portfolio?

Yes. We can work with you consistently across several affected blocks, applying the same disciplined process to each rather than you having to find and brief a different specialist for every building.

04

Do we lose control of the client relationship if we bring Wrightwood in?

No. We work alongside you, not in place of you — you retain the overall management instruction and client relationship, while we take on the specific technical and project-management workload for the remediation process.

05

What's the best first step if a client is asking about cladding risk?

Gather what building and fire safety information already exists and get in touch — we can give you an initial, honest view of whether a formal assessment is likely to be needed before you have to commit your client to anything. See our guide to whether a building needs a FRAEW.
